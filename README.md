# Mindmint prototype

A playable web prototype of Mindmint, a focused, child-safe practice app for ages 4 to 13.

Its advantage is transparent progress, useful parent guidance, and activities that connect game play with everyday life.

## What's inside

- 18 short games, 3 per skill. Tap 🎮 All games to play any of them, any time: Memory, Focus, Numbers, Noticing, Logic, Words
- Adaptive levels (two right in a row: harder; one miss: easier), each written as a plain sentence
- Spaced review with a daily trick warm-up, family profiles (up to 4 children), glow garden rewards
- Parent area behind a simple maths gate: session notes, weekly summary, everyday activities, settings
- Feedback form that parents send by WhatsApp or copy and paste

## Running it

It is a single self-contained `index.html` file. Open it in a browser, or deploy the folder as a static site (no build step).

All data stays in the visitor's browser (localStorage). There are no ads, accounts, analytics or third-party trackers.

## Maths tricks (Vedic maths)

A separate track of thirty-six tricks in five sections (Starter, Vedic tricks, Maths for everyday, Shapes, Champion), each section unlocking in order: subtract from 100/1000 (Nikhilam), numbers ending in 5 (Ekadhikena Purvena), times 5/12/15/75, the trick of 9, divisibility tests, 2-digit × 2-digit (Urdhva Tiryagbhyam), squares near 100 (Yavadunam), percent tricks, fractions to decimals, quick averages, square roots and cube roots (Vilokanam), big times tables, multiplying near 100 (Nikhilam), cubes of 2-digit numbers (Anurupyena), dividing by 9, prime factors, BODMAS, find the zero (Sunyam Samya Samuchaye), fraction cutting, fraction sums, ratio and sharing, linear equations, mean/median/mode, reading big numbers (lakh/crore), quick adding, number pegs (memory), Vestanam (testing ÷7, 13, 19, 29), tidying up algebra, and square roots to 1 decimal place. Each trick has an animated lesson, 6 levels, a number keypad, step-by-step replay after a mistake, and a 60-second speed round from level 3.

Shapes (geometry, drawn on screen): four-sided shapes, area and perimeter, circles (π = 22/7), 3D shapes (faces, edges, vertices, volume, surface area), tricky composite shapes, and angles.
